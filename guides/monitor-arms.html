Monitor Arms

Best Monitor Arms for a Gaming or Streaming Desk (Budget to Premium)

If there's one upgrade that changes a desk setup more than people expect, it's a monitor arm. Pulling your screen off a stand and onto an arm frees up desk space for a mic, a keyboard tray, or just room to breathe — and it lets you actually dial in eye-level height and distance instead of stacking your monitor on a couple of old textbooks. If your neck or shoulders ache after a long session, this is usually the first fix worth trying, before you spend money on a fancier chair.

This guide breaks down three solid picks across different budgets, plus what actually matters when you're choosing one — because monitor arms aren't a "buy the cheapest one" category. A bad arm droops, wobbles when you type, or simply can't hold your monitor's weight.

What actually matters when buying a monitor arm

Weight capacity. Every arm lists a supported weight range (usually in pounds or kg) — check your exact monitor's weight, not just its screen size. A 32-inch ultrawide can weigh noticeably more than a 27-inch flat panel.

VESA compatibility. Almost all modern monitors support VESA mounting (75x75mm or 100x100mm hole patterns on the back), but it's worth a 30-second check before you buy anything.

Desk mount type. Most arms clamp to the desk edge or use a grommet mount through a drilled hole. Clamp mounts are far more common and don't require modifying your desk.

Gas spring vs. friction arm. Gas-spring arms let you adjust height with one hand and a smooth motion — better for frequent repositioning. Friction arms are cheaper but need more force to move and can droop over time as the tension wears.

Budget pick

Huanuo HNSS6 ~$40

For a first monitor arm, the Huanuo HNSS6 is a genuinely reasonable starting point. It's a gas-spring single-monitor arm that handles most standard monitors without issue, and the installation is beginner-friendly — clamp it to the desk, attach the VESA plate, and you're adjusting height within about fifteen minutes. It won't have the buttery-smooth long-term durability of a premium arm, but for someone testing whether they even like using an arm versus a stand, it's a low-risk way to find out.

Ergotron LX ~$209

The Ergotron LX is the arm most enthusiast desk-setup channels eventually recommend, and for good reason — it's built to last, the height adjustment is effortless, and the cable management channel built into the arm actually works instead of being an afterthought. It supports a wide range of monitor sizes and weights, and Ergotron's reputation for durability means this is realistically a "buy it once" purchase rather than something you'll replace in a year. If your budget allows for one upgrade in this category, this is the one most people land on.

Premium pick

Ergotron HX ~$345

For larger, heavier monitors — think 32-inch-plus panels or monitors north of 20 lbs — the Ergotron HX is built specifically for that job, rated to support up to 42 lbs. It has the same smooth one-handed adjustment as the LX but with a sturdier build to match the extra weight. If you're running a large ultrawide or a heavier gaming monitor with a lot of HDR backlighting hardware inside, this is the safer buy over the LX, which can feel undersized for genuinely heavy panels.

Which one should you actually get?

If you're not sure a monitor arm is for you yet, start with the Huanuo and see how it changes your desk. If you already know you want a permanent upgrade and your monitor is a standard 24–27 inch panel, the Ergotron LX is the one to buy once and stop thinking about. Only step up to the HX if your monitor is genuinely large or heavy — for most gaming and streaming monitors, the LX has plenty of headroom.

Quick FAQ

Do I need to drill into my desk? No — nearly all of these use a clamp mount that grips the desk edge. A grommet mount (through a hole) is usually an alternative option if your desk already has one.

Will it work with my desk's thickness? Check the clamp's stated desk thickness range before buying, especially with thicker standing desks — most clamps handle up to about 3.5 inches, but it varies by model.

Can I mount two monitors on a single-monitor arm? No — these are single-arm mounts. Dual-monitor setups need a dual arm or two separate single arms mounted side by side.
